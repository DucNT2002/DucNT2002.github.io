Cloud infrastructure for Samsung’s push notification ecosystem.

SMP supports targeted notifications, campaign scheduling, and delivery analytics through Firebase Cloud Messaging and Samsung Push Platform. As a DevOps engineer on a 20-person team, I worked on infrastructure design, provisioning, delivery, and production operations.

  • Infrastructure design: contributed high-level architecture and cost estimates, then provisioned AWS resources with Terraform.
  • Production workloads: operated three EKS clusters across Singapore, Oregon, and N. Virginia, with approximately eight nodes per region and separate development and staging environments.
  • Delivery automation: built CI/CD with CodePipeline and CodeBuild; automated recurring operational tasks with Lambda.
  • Operational visibility: configured CloudWatch Container Insights, CloudWatch Agent, and Fluent Bit for monitoring, logs, and incident alerting.

Design and provision

Worked on architecture and cost planning for a cloud-native messaging platform. Provisioned EKS, ECS, ALB, CloudFront, S3, VPC endpoints, ECR, RDS, and supporting AWS services through Terraform.

Traffic and platform scope

Sustained traffic of approximately 3–4 million HTTP requests per minute reached ALBs across all three regions, primarily periodic reporting from Samsung devices. Within that total, approximately 300–400K requests per minute initiated push messages from other Samsung platforms and users. These figures describe inbound HTTP requests, not successful notification deliveries.

The supported AWS environment had approximately $90K in monthly spend; this describes operating scope, not savings.

Deliver and operate

Maintained distinct environments for controlled delivery. Managed Kubernetes workloads with attention to availability and fault tolerance, and used AWS-native delivery services to automate the release workflow.

Optimize and document

Worked on resource rightsizing, scheduling, and serverless offloading to reduce infrastructure spend. Maintained incident-response, deployment, and recovery runbooks so operational procedures were repeatable.
